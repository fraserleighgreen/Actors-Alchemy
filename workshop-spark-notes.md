# Actors Alchemy Workshop Spark Notes

## Core Approach: Teacher + Collaborator

**As teacher:** bring structure, professional context, practical tools, clear standards and honest feedback.

**As collaborator:** respect the performer’s instincts, ask questions, experiment together and leave artistic ownership with the student.

### Shared Workshop Journey

1. **Arrive** — welcome, expectations and a safe working room.
2. **Reveal** — introduce the central idea or industry reality.
3. **Practise** — learn through doing.
4. **Refine** — receive feedback, adjust and try again.
5. **Reflect** — name what changed and why.
6. **Continue** — leave with a repeatable tool or next step.

### Shared Feedback Language

- What I observed
- What communicated
- What might be getting in the way
- What we could try
- What the performer wants to keep

## 01. The Mock Audition

**Purpose:** Demystify the audition room and build adaptable, connected performance under pressure.

**Length:** 2 hours

**Prepare:** Students bring 32 bars. Arrange pianist or backing tracks. Set the movement level in advance.

**Flow:**

- Welcome and fictional casting brief
- Audition-room etiquette and expectations
- First-round song auditions
- Group reflection on pressure and choices
- Mock movement call
- Selected recalls with redirection
- Debrief and personal next steps

**Teach:** Room etiquette, preparation, communication, redirection and recovery.

**Collaborate:** Ask what the performer intended, what pressure changed and what adjustment they want to test.

**Takeaway:** Personal audition checklist.

## 02. The Mindset Mentor

**Working subtitle:** *Working With Pressure*

**Purpose:** Help performers understand their response to pressure and create a personal regulation routine.

**Length:** 1 hour

**Flow:**

- Reframe nerves as information, not failure
- Map trigger → thought → body → behaviour
- Test grounding, breathing, focus and release tools
- Apply a tool to a short performance exercise
- Build a personal pre-performance routine
- Reflect and commit to one practice

**Teach:** Accessible stress-response ideas and several practical tools.

**Collaborate:** Let each student decide what genuinely serves them; ask “What changed?” rather than “Did that fix you?”

**Takeaway:** A three-stage routine for before, during and after performance.

**Boundary:** Performance education, not therapy. No required personal disclosure.

## 03. The Company Code

**Purpose:** Build professional judgement, communication and company awareness—not simply impose a list of rules.

**Length:** 1 hour

**Flow:**

- What makes somebody employable again?
- Map the company structure and chain of communication
- Solve small-group professional dilemmas
- Discuss contracts, pay, exposure, scams and promotion
- Build a shared company code
- Choose one personal professional commitment

**Scenario ideas:**

- Rehearsal material is posted online
- A friend is repeatedly late
- A note feels unclear or uncomfortable
- An injured performer fears losing work
- An unpaid “exposure” offer arrives
- A suspicious online casting asks for money or unusual material

**Teach:** Boundaries, responsibilities, escalation routes and contractual awareness.

**Collaborate:** Let students debate the grey areas and explain their reasoning before offering your perspective.

**Takeaway:** Ask: What happened? Is anyone unsafe? Is this mine to resolve? Who should I approach? What should I record?

**Check before delivery:** Current Equity rates and platform guidance.

## 04. Self-Tape Synthesis

**Purpose:** Create clear, truthful and technically usable tapes without letting equipment or perfectionism overwhelm the acting.

**Length:** 2 hours

**Prepare:** Supply a short scene. Students bring a phone and, where possible, a tripod. Work in actor-reader pairs.

**Flow:**

- Explain what casting needs from a tape
- Demonstrate framing, eyeline, sound, light and slating
- Introduce an efficient cold-reading process
- Record take one
- Review using the shared feedback language
- Redirect and record take two
- Cover file naming, trimming and delivery
- Finish with singer-showreel considerations

**Teach:** A reliable minimum technical standard and a repeatable preparation process.

**Collaborate:** Treat actor, reader and facilitator as a creative team; compare takes and let the performer assess what communicates.

**Takeaway:** Self-tape checklist plus two contrasting takes.

**Boundary:** Any promotional use requires separate, specific written consent. Participation must never depend on consent; under-18s need appropriate guardian/school permissions.

## 05. Ship Shape: Cruise 101

**Purpose:** Give an honest picture of winning cruise work and sustaining life within the contract.

**Length:** 2 hours

**Versions:** Singer focus or dancer focus, plus a shared professional-life section.

**Flow:**

- Map the cruise industry and contract types
- Explain the audition journey
- Run a mock vocal or dance call
- Give notes, redirect and repeat
- Explore rehearsals, medicals, visas, cabins and schedules
- Work through a company-chemistry scenario
- Finish with honest Q&A and suitability reflection

**Teach:** Performance expectations and the operational realities of ship life.

**Collaborate:** Help students consider whether cruise work matches their needs, priorities and temperament.

**Takeaway:** Cruise-readiness audit covering performance, administration, wellbeing, communal living and professionalism.

**Boundary:** Separate personal experience from facts that vary by employer, ship or contract.

---

## 06. The Q&A

**Purpose:** Turn uncertainty into better questions, clearer distinctions and practical next steps.

**Length:** Flexible; standalone or an add-on.

**Flow:**

- Collect anonymous questions before and during the session
- Group them into casting, agents, contracts, confidence, company life and leaving training
- Label answers as:
  - what is generally true
  - what varies
  - what happened in my experience
- Close with: “What will you do differently now?”

**Teach:** Correct myths, explain terminology and acknowledge the limits of your expertise.

**Collaborate:** Invite useful perspectives from students and other practitioners; show that industry knowledge can be contextual and evolving.

**Takeaway:** A personal next-step card.

**Boundary:** Signpost legal, medical and mental-health questions beyond your professional competence.

## Suggested Programme Order

1. **The Mindset Mentor** — understand yourself under pressure.
2. **Self-Tape Synthesis** — work truthfully and independently.
3. **The Mock Audition** — apply the skills live.
4. **The Company Code** — enter the professional community.
5. **Ship Shape** — apply the learning to a specific sector.
6. **The Q&A** — integrate, reflect and plan the transition.

## Questions to Answer When Expanding Each Workshop

- Who is it for: year group, discipline and experience level?
- What should students know, feel or do differently afterwards?
- What pre-work or materials are required?
- How many students can participate meaningfully?
- Where will every student practise rather than only observe?
- What reflection or resource will they leave with?
- What needs adapting for access, mixed ability or online delivery?
- What consent, safeguarding or current-industry checks are required?
- How will the school and students know the workshop was useful?
